Reading I2C off the MSPM0L2228 LaunchPad

The firmware runs an I2C controller and an I2C peripheral on the same chip, on two different sets of pins. You'll join them into one bus with two jumper wires, clip a Saleae Logic 8 onto the peripheral side, and decode the conversation in Logic 2.

Target

TI LaunchPad LP-MSPM0L2228, pre-flashed, plus its USB cable for power.

Jumpers

Two female-to-female jumper wires to join the controller and peripheral pins.

Analyzer

Saleae Logic 8 with three grabber clips, and Logic 2 on your laptop.

1

Find the I2C pins on the pinout

I2C uses just two lines: SDA (data) and SCL (clock). This board has two sets of them. The firmware uses one set as the controller (it starts every transfer) and the other as the peripheral (it answers).

SideSignalBoard pinWhere
ControllerI2C_C_SCLPA15Upper-left header
ControllerI2C_C_SDAPA18Lower-left header, below GND
PeripheralI2C_P_SDAPB1Upper-right header, below GND
PeripheralI2C_P_SCLPB0Upper-right header, below PB1
2

Jump the two I2C buses together

Right now the controller and peripheral pins aren't connected, so the controller has nobody to talk to. Two jumper wires turn them into a single bus. Do this with the board unplugged.

  1. SDA jumper: connect PA18 (I2C_C_SDA, left) to PB1 (I2C_P_SDA, right).
  2. SCL jumper: connect PA15 (I2C_C_SCL, left) to PB0 (I2C_P_SCL, right).
Match like with like. Unlike UART, I2C lines are never crossed. SDA always goes to SDA and SCL to SCL. Swapping them gives you a bus that never works.
3

Clip the analyzer onto the peripheral side

Since the jumpers make both sides the same electrical bus, you could probe either one. We use the peripheral side (the right-hand header) so everyone's setup looks the same. Keep the Logic 8 unplugged from your laptop while you wire.

  1. Clip the Logic 8's GND lead to the GND pin at the top of the right header. Do this first, every time.
  2. Clip Channel 4 to PB1 (SDA).
  3. Clip Channel 5 to PB0 (SCL).
  4. Plug the LaunchPad into USB so it powers up, then plug the Logic 8 into your laptop.

PB1 and PB0 will each have two things on them: a jumper wire and an analyzer clip. Clip the grabber onto the exposed header pin next to the jumper's connector, or onto the jumper's metal where it meets the pin, so both stay seated.

Do not connect to the red power pins. 3V3 and 5V are supply rails, not signals. Clipping a probe, jumper, or ground lead onto them risks shorting the board's supply.
4

Set up the capture in Logic 2

Open the Logic 8 settings panel (the device icon just right of the play button).

  • Digital: make sure channels 4 and 5 are on. The screenshot still has 0–3 enabled from the UART and SPI labs; that's fine, or click Clear and turn on just 4 and 5.
  • Sample rate: 50 MS/s. I2C usually runs at 100 kHz or 400 kHz, so this is far more than enough.
  • Analog: leave everything cleared.
  • Capture mode: Looping keeps recording until you press stop.
5

Add the I2C analyzer

Open the Analyzers panel, click +, and pick I2C. Analyzers from the earlier labs can stay or be deleted; they won't interfere.

I2C only needs two settings:

SettingValue
SDA04. Channel 4
SCL05. Channel 5

Keep Stream to terminal checked, then click Save.

7-bit vs. 8-bit addresses. Logic 2 shows addresses as 7-bit numbers. Some datasheets list an 8-bit address that includes the read/write bit, which is the 7-bit value shifted left by one. For example, 7-bit 0x48 appears as 0x90 (write) and 0x91 (read).
6

Capture and read the output

  1. Press the play button to start recording.
  2. Press the board's reset button (NRST, top edge of the board) so the firmware starts its I2C traffic from the beginning.
  3. Wait a few seconds, then press stop.
  4. Read the Data table in the Analyzers panel, or open the Terminal view.

Each I2C transaction reads like a sentence. Zoom into a burst and you'll see Logic 2 mark each part:

PieceWhat it means
StartSDA falls while SCL is high. The controller is taking the bus.
Address + R/WWhich peripheral is being called, and whether the controller will write or read.
ACK / NAKAfter every byte, the receiver pulls SDA low to acknowledge (ACK). NAK means nobody answered or the transfer is ending.
Data bytesThe payload, each followed by its own ACK or NAK.
StopSDA rises while SCL is high. The bus is free again.
7

Check what you captured

  • Find the address: note the peripheral address from the Data table. On an unknown board, this is the first clue to what chip you're talking to.
  • Confirm ACKs: if the address byte is ACKed, the jumpers are working and the peripheral is answering.
  • Check the bus speed: hover over one SCL period to read its frequency. You'll most likely see about 100 kHz or 400 kHz.
  • Separate reads from writes: writes send data to the peripheral; reads have the peripheral sending data back on the same SDA wire.
8

If something looks wrong

SDA and SCL stay low the whole time

I2C lines idle high through pull-up resistors. If both are stuck low, a jumper may be on the wrong pin or touching GND. Unplug the board and recheck both jumpers against step 2.

The address shows up, but every byte is NAKed

The controller is talking but nobody is answering. The jumpers are probably swapped (SDA to SCL) or one is loose. Confirm PA18 goes to PB1 and PA15 goes to PB0.

No decoded data at all

Check the analyzer settings: SDA must be channel 4 and SCL channel 5. If they're reversed, Logic 2 can't find any start conditions. Also press reset during the capture in case the firmware only talks at startup.

Waveform looks noisy or random

The ground lead is missing or loose. Re-seat it on the GND pin at the top of the right header.

A jumper keeps falling off when I clip the probe

Clip the grabber onto the exposed metal of the header pin rather than on top of the jumper housing, or use a jumper with a longer connector so there's room for both.
