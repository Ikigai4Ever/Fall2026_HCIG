Reading UART TX off the MSPM0L2228 LaunchPad

The board is already flashed and talking. Your job is to listen: clip a Saleae Logic 8 onto the board's transmit line, capture the traffic in Logic 2, and decode it back into readable text. Two wires, one analyzer, no soldering.

Target

TI LaunchPad LP-MSPM0L2228, pre-flashed, plus its USB cable for power.

Analyzer

Saleae Logic 8 with its wire harness and two grabber clips.

Software

Saleae Logic 2 installed on your laptop.

1

Find the TX pin on the pinout

UART has two data lines, but the board only sends on one of them. Since we're just eavesdropping on what the firmware prints, we only need UART_TX, which is pin PB2 on the right-hand header. UART_RX on PB3 is the board's input and can be ignored today.

Why no crossover? If you were wiring a USB-to-serial adapter, TX would go to the adapter's RX. A logic analyzer only listens, so its channel goes straight onto the line you want to watch: PB2 to channel 0.
2

Wire it up

Do this with the Logic 8 unplugged from your laptop.

  1. Clip the Logic 8's GND lead to any GND pin on the LaunchPad. Do this first, every time.
  2. Clip the Channel 0 signal lead to PB2.
  3. Plug the LaunchPad into USB so it powers up, then plug the Logic 8 into your laptop.
Do not connect to the red power pins. 3V3 and 5V are supply rails, not signals. Clipping a probe or ground lead onto them risks shorting the board's supply.
No ground, no data. Without a shared ground, the analyzer has no reference for "high" and "low" and you'll see noise or nothing at all. Channel leads are color-coded to match the channel colors in Logic 2; check the label on your Logic 8 so you don't mistake a signal lead for a ground lead.
3

Set up the capture in Logic 2

Open Logic 2 and click the device settings button on the top bar (the device icon just right of the play button) to open the Logic 8 panel.

  • Digital: enable channel 0 only. Clicking Clear first, then 0, is the quickest way.
  • Sample rate: 100 MS/s is plenty. A good rule is at least 4×, ideally 10×, the baud rate; at 115200 baud even a few MS/s would work, so there's lots of margin.
  • Analog: leave everything cleared. We don't need voltage traces for this, and extra channels eat buffer memory.
  • Capture mode: Looping is fine for this lab. It keeps recording until you press stop, holding the most recent data in the memory buffer.
4

Add the Async Serial analyzer

UART is "asynchronous serial" in Logic 2's vocabulary. Open the Analyzers panel on the right sidebar, click +, and pick Async Serial.

Match the settings to how the firmware configured its UART. For this board that's the common "115200 8N1" setup:

Keep Stream to terminal checked so the decoded text shows up as a live console, then click Save.

5

Capture and read the output

  1. Press the play button to start recording.
  2. Press the board's reset button (NRST, top edge of the board) so the firmware starts over and re-sends anything it prints at boot.
  3. Wait a few seconds, then press stop.
  4. Open the Terminal view (bottom-right icon on the sidebar) to read the decoded text, or check the Data table in the Analyzers panel for byte-by-byte results with timestamps.

On the waveform, zoom into a burst on channel 0. The line sits high when idle, drops low for a start bit, carries 8 data bits, and returns high for the stop bit. Logic 2 draws the decoded character above each frame.

6

Verify the baud rate yourself

On an unknown device you won't be handed the baud rate, so it's worth confirming it from the waveform. Zoom in on a frame and measure the narrowest low or high pulse; that's one bit.

1 ÷ 115200 ≈ 8.68 µs per bit

Hover over a single-bit pulse and Logic 2 shows its width. If it's about 8.7 µs, you're at 115200. If you measure something like 104 µs, the rate is 9600 instead. Take 1 ÷ bit width and round to the nearest standard rate (9600, 19200, 38400, 57600, 115200…), then update the analyzer.

7

If something looks wrong

Channel 0 is flat and never changes

Check the clip is on PB2, not PB3 or a neighboring pin. Confirm the board is powered (LEDs on) and press reset while capturing, since some firmware only prints once at startup.

Decoded output is garbage or shows framing errors

The bit rate is almost certainly wrong. Measure it as in step 6. Also double-check 8 bits, no parity, 1 stop bit.

Waveform looks noisy or random

The ground lead is missing or loose. Re-seat it on a GND pin. Keep leads short and away from the USB cable.

Signal is idle low instead of high

Standard UART idles high. If yours idles low, you may be on the wrong pin; if the pin is right, try setting Signal inversion to Inverted.

Logic 2 doesn't see the Logic 8

Unplug and replug the analyzer's USB cable, try a different port, and close any other Logic 2 windows.
