Reading SPI off the MSPM0L2228 LaunchPad

The board is already flashed and talking over SPI. Your job is to listen: clip a Saleae Logic 8 onto the clock, data, and chip-select lines, capture the traffic in Logic 2, and decode the bytes. Four wires, one analyzer, no soldering.

Target

TI LaunchPad LP-MSPM0L2228, pre-flashed, plus its USB cable for power.

Analyzer

Saleae Logic 8 with its wire harness and four grabber clips.

Software

Saleae Logic 2 installed on your laptop.

1

Find the SPI pins on the pinout

SPI is a synchronous bus: the controller drives a clock, and data is sampled on that clock's edges. That means we need more wires than UART. For this lab you'll watch three signals plus ground:

SignalWhat it doesBoard pin
SPI_PICO (MOSI)Data from the controller (the MSPM0) to the peripheralPB8
SPI_SCLKClock that times every bitPA17
SPI_CSChip select; goes low to mark the start and end of a transferPA26
SPI_POCI (MISO)Data coming back from the peripheral (optional today)PB7

PB8 and PB7 are near the top of the right header, SPI_CS (PA26) is at the bottom of the right header, and SPI_SCLK (PA17) is on the lower-left header.

PICO and POCI? These are the newer names for MOSI and MISO: Peripheral In / Controller Out and Peripheral Out / Controller In. Logic 2 still uses the old names, so PICO = MOSI and POCI = MISO.
2

Wire it up

Do this with the Logic 8 unplugged from your laptop.

  1. Clip the Logic 8's GND lead to a GND pin on the LaunchPad. Do this first, every time.
  2. Clip Channel 1 to PB8 (MOSI).
  3. Clip Channel 2 to PA17 (SCLK).
  4. Clip Channel 3 to PA26 (CS).
  5. Plug the LaunchPad into USB so it powers up, then plug the Logic 8 into your laptop.
Do not connect to the red power pins. 3V3 and 5V are supply rails, not signals. Clipping a probe or ground lead onto them risks shorting the board's supply.
Want the replies too? Clip Channel 4 to PB7 (MISO), enable channel 4 in step 3, and pick it as MISO in step 4. The rest of the guide works the same.
3

Set up the capture in Logic 2

Open Logic 2 and click the device settings button (the device icon just right of the play button) to open the Logic 8 panel.

  • Digital: click Clear, then enable channels 1, 2, and 3 (plus 4 if you're capturing MISO).
  • Sample rate: 50 MS/s. Sample at least 4× the SPI clock frequency, ideally 10×. 50 MS/s comfortably covers clocks up to a few MHz, which is typical for this kind of board.
  • Analog: leave everything cleared.
  • Capture mode: Looping keeps recording until you press stop.
4

Add the SPI analyzer

Open the Analyzers panel on the right sidebar, click +, and pick SPI. If you still have an Async Serial analyzer from the UART lab, you can delete it.

Map each role to the channel you clipped, and use the standard SPI Mode 0 settings:

SettingValue
MOSI01. Channel 1
MISONone (or Channel 4)
Clock02. Channel 2
Enable03. Channel 3
Significant BitMSB first
Bits per Transfer8
Clock StateLow when inactive (CPOL = 0)
Clock PhaseValid on leading edge (CPHA = 0)
Enable LineActive low

Keep Stream to terminal checked, then click Save.

5

Capture and read the output

  1. Press the play button to start recording.
  2. Press the board's reset button (NRST, top edge of the board) so the firmware re-sends anything it transmits at startup.
  3. Wait a few seconds, then press stop.
  4. Check the Data table in the Analyzers panel for each byte with its timestamp, or open the Terminal view to read it as a stream.

Zoom into a burst on the waveform. You should see CS drop low, SCLK toggle eight times per byte while MOSI changes between pulses, then CS return high. Logic 2 labels each byte above the MOSI trace.

Hex or text? SPI often carries raw commands and register values instead of readable text. If the terminal looks like gibberish, switch the display format to hex or ASCII from the analyzer's menu (the ⋮ next to it) and see which makes sense.
6

Verify the settings yourself

On an unknown device nobody hands you the SPI mode, so learn to read it from the waveform.

  • Clock polarity (CPOL): look at SCLK while CS is high. Resting low means CPOL = 0; resting high means CPOL = 1.
  • Clock phase (CPHA): MOSI should be stable on the edge where it's sampled. If data changes before the first clock edge, it's CPHA = 0. If it changes on the first edge and is stable on the second, it's CPHA = 1.
  • Clock speed: hover over one SCLK period to read its frequency. Your sample rate should be at least 4× that number.
7

If something looks wrong

No decoded bytes at all

Make sure the analyzer's Clock is set to the channel actually clipped to PA17. Check that CS is on PA26 and goes low during bursts; if it never does, try setting Enable to None to confirm data decodes without it.

Bytes decode but the values look wrong

The SPI mode or bit order is off. Recheck CPOL and CPHA from the waveform (step 6) and confirm MSB first.

Byte boundaries look shifted

The analyzer isn't seeing CS correctly, so it doesn't know where each transfer starts. Re-seat the channel 3 clip and confirm Enable is set to active low.

Clock looks irregular or has missing pulses

The sample rate is too low for the clock speed, or ground is loose. Raise the sample rate and re-seat the ground clip.

Logic 2 doesn't see the Logic 8

Unplug and replug the analyzer's USB cable, try a different port, and close any other Logic 2 windows.
